# Marketplace Growth & Seller Analytics

An end-to-end marketplace analytics project focused on growth, buyer retention, seller performance, and marketplace risk.

This project analyzes a simulated two-sided marketplace from July 2025 through June 2026 using BigQuery SQL, Python, and Tableau. The goal was to go beyond topline growth and evaluate whether the marketplace was growing in a healthy and sustainable way.

## Executive Summary

This project looks at how a simulated two-sided marketplace performed from July 2025 through June 2026, with a focus on growth, buyer behavior, seller performance, retention, and marketplace risk.

Over the year, monthly GMV grew from about $44K to more than $106K, an increase of roughly 141%. A growing share of that revenue came from returning buyers, which suggests the marketplace was building a more established customer base.

At the same time, the analysis uncovered two areas that could become problems if they are not addressed. Newer buyer cohorts were much less likely to return after their first month, and nearly half of total GMV came from just 10 sellers.

Overall, the marketplace is growing quickly, but the next stage of growth should focus less on simply bringing in more users and more on improving early buyer retention and building a broader base of successful sellers.

## Key Findings

- **Marketplace growth was strong:** Monthly GMV increased from **$44,250 in July 2025 to $106,611 in June 2026**, representing approximately **141% growth** over the analysis period.
- **Returning buyers became increasingly important:** By June 2026, returning buyers generated about **69% of monthly GMV** and generally purchased more frequently than new buyers.
- **Early retention weakened over time:** Month-1 retention declined from **62.62% for the July 2025 cohort to 24.36% for the May 2026 cohort**.
- **Category performance varied by metric:** Electronics generated the most GMV, while Trading Cards and Collectibles had the strongest repeat purchase behavior.
- **Seller concentration creates risk:** The top 10 sellers accounted for **47.49% of total marketplace GMV**.

## Business Problem

The goal of this project was to evaluate whether the marketplace was growing in a healthy and sustainable way.

Rather than looking only at revenue growth, I focused on five areas of marketplace health:

1. Growth
2. Buyer activity
3. Seller activity
4. Customer retention
5. Marketplace risk

I defined the KPIs before beginning the analysis so that the work stayed focused on the business questions rather than simply exploring whatever metrics were available.

### KPI Definitions

- **GMV:** Total value of merchandise sold through completed orders.
- **Active Buyers:** Buyers who completed at least one purchase during the analysis period.
- **Active Sellers:** Sellers who created a listing or completed a sale during the period.
- **Repeat Purchase Rate:** Percentage of buyers who completed at least two purchases.
- **Seller Sell-Through Rate:** Percentage of eligible listings that resulted in a sale.
- **Top 10 Seller GMV Share:** Percentage of total GMV generated by the marketplace's 10 highest-GMV sellers.

These metrics were chosen to balance growth with marketplace health. GMV shows whether the business is expanding, while retention, sell-through, and seller concentration help show whether that growth is sustainable.

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Focus on early buyer retention

Returning buyers became increasingly important to marketplace growth, but newer buyer cohorts were much less likely to return soon after their first purchase.

Month-1 retention fell from **62.62% for the July 2025 cohort to 24.36% for the May 2026 cohort**.

I would prioritize the experience immediately following a buyer's first purchase. Potential tests could include personalized recommendations, follow-up offers, reminders about previously purchased categories or sellers, and incentives designed specifically to encourage a second purchase.

The goal would be to convert more newly acquired buyers into repeat customers rather than relying primarily on continued acquisition to maintain growth.

### 2. Reduce dependence on the largest sellers

The top 10 sellers generated **47.49% of total marketplace GMV**, creating a meaningful concentration risk.

I would approach this from two directions: retain the marketplace's strongest sellers while also helping promising mid-tier sellers grow.

Seller education, performance insights, onboarding support, and targeted seller-development programs could help create a broader group of meaningful GMV contributors.

The goal is not to reduce the success of top sellers. It is to reduce the marketplace's dependence on a relatively small group of them.

### 3. Use different strategies by category

Different categories contributed value in different ways.

**Electronics generated the highest GMV**, while **Trading Cards and Collectibles showed the strongest repeat purchase behavior**.

Rather than using the same growth strategy across every category, I would tailor the approach to how each category performs. High-GMV categories may offer strong revenue and seller-growth opportunities, while high-repeat categories may be especially useful for retention and recurring engagement strategies.

### 4. Target support toward low-performing sellers

Marketplace-wide sell-through was healthy, and most sellers who created listings eventually made a sale. However, a small group of sellers had substantially weaker sell-through rates.

Those sellers were spread across multiple categories, and their pricing was not consistently higher than other sellers.

Because the current data does not identify a clear marketplace-wide cause, I would focus on seller-level support rather than broad policy changes. Additional listing guidance, merchandising feedback, and seller performance tools could help identify what is limiting their conversion.

## Methodology

I approached the project as an end-to-end marketplace analytics workflow, beginning with the business questions and KPI definitions before moving into data preparation and analysis.

The raw marketplace data was loaded into **BigQuery**, where I validated table relationships, checked for missing and inconsistent values, standardized fields, and created a cleaned analytical layer.

I then used **SQL** to calculate the core marketplace KPIs and analyze growth, retention, buyer behavior, category performance, seller performance, and marketplace concentration.

I used **Python in Google Colab** to validate SQL results, calculate additional trends, reshape cohort data, and create exploratory visualizations.

The final findings were brought into **Tableau Public**, where I built an executive dashboard focused on marketplace growth, buyer retention, seller performance, and concentration risk.

### Analytical Workflow

**Business Problem → KPI Definition → Data Validation → Data Cleaning → SQL Analysis → Python Analysis → Tableau Dashboard → Business Recommendations**

## Data Preparation & Validation

The marketplace data was organized across five related tables:

- Users
- Sellers
- Listings
- Orders
- Order Items

Before calculating KPIs, I validated the relationships between the tables and checked for data-quality issues that could affect the analysis.

Issues identified included:

- Inconsistent capitalization in order statuses
- Inconsistent capitalization and spacing in product categories
- Missing user state values
- Unrealistic listing prices
- Potential key and relationship issues

I also checked for:

- Duplicate primary IDs
- Missing foreign keys
- Orphaned buyer, seller, listing, and order records
- Order totals that did not match item-level totals
- Invalid buyer and seller relationships
- Inconsistent order and listing statuses
- Impossible transaction timing

The raw data was preserved unchanged. I created a separate cleaned analytical layer in BigQuery so that transformations did not overwrite the source data.

For questionable listing prices, I preserved the original value, created a cleaned version of the field, and added an anomaly flag rather than deleting the entire record.

## Data Model

The analysis used five related marketplace tables:

- **Users** — buyer and seller account information
- **Sellers** — seller-specific account and status information
- **Listings** — marketplace listings, categories, prices, and listing status
- **Orders** — one record per order, including buyer, seller, date, status, and order total
- **Order Items** — individual listings contained within each order

The core analytical relationship followed:

**Buyer → Order → Order Item → Listing → Seller**

Understanding the grain of each table was important for avoiding duplicate counts and ensuring each KPI was calculated at the correct level of detail.

## Limitations

This project uses a **simulated marketplace dataset**, so the results demonstrate the analytical process rather than describe the performance of a real company.

The dataset also does not include several factors that could help explain why certain patterns occurred, including:

- Marketing campaigns and acquisition sources
- Promotions and discounts
- Listing views or search impressions
- Listing quality
- Favorites or engagement activity
- Customer service interactions
- Seller fulfillment metrics
- Product-level merchandising information

Buyer cohorts are based on the first purchase observed within the available analysis window. If earlier transaction history were unavailable in a real-world dataset, customers who purchased before the observation window could incorrectly appear to be new buyers.

Because of these limitations, findings such as declining early retention and weaker seller performance should be treated as areas for further investigation rather than proof of a specific cause.

## What I Would Investigate Next

The first priority would be understanding **why early buyer retention declined**.

I would compare retained and non-retained buyers using additional factors such as:

- Acquisition source
- First-purchase category
- Promotional usage
- First-order experience
- Seller quality
- Time to second purchase

I would also track **Top 10 Seller GMV Share over time** and identify promising mid-tier sellers that could grow into larger marketplace contributors.

For seller performance, I would add behavioral data such as listing views, favorites, price changes, listing age, promotions, and fulfillment metrics. That would make it possible to investigate why certain sellers have lower sell-through rates instead of simply identifying that the difference exists.

## Conclusion

This project showed that marketplace growth can look healthy at a high level while still hiding risks underneath the surface.

GMV increased substantially throughout the year, and returning buyers became an increasingly important source of revenue. At the same time, declining early retention and the amount of GMV concentrated among a relatively small group of sellers revealed areas where future growth could become vulnerable.

The biggest takeaway from the project was that no single metric tells the whole story. Looking at growth alongside retention, buyer behavior, category performance, and seller activity provided a much clearer view of marketplace health and led to more useful business recommendations.

The analysis ultimately suggests that the marketplace has strong momentum, but sustaining that growth will depend on converting more new buyers into repeat customers and developing a broader base of successful sellers.
